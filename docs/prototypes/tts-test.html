How spaced repetition helps you remember what you read

Test article · 3 mins

Most of what we read fades within a few days. That is not a failure of attention; it is simply how memory works when nothing asks us to recall an idea again.

Spaced repetition fixes this by bringing an idea back just before you would forget it. Each successful recall pushes the next reminder further into the future.

Highlights make a natural unit for this. They are short, they are already chosen by you, and they carry the context of the book or article they came from.

Listening is another way in. Hearing a passage read aloud, while the words light up on screen, can help on a commute or when your eyes are tired.

A good listening mode stays out of the way. It follows along as it reads, lets you glance ahead without losing your place, and brings you back when you are ready.

The controls should be few. Play and pause, a way to go back a little, a way to skip ahead, and a choice of voice and speed are usually all that is needed.

Everything else can live one tap away, in a sheet that slides up from the bottom of the screen and disappears again when you are done with it.

That is the whole idea. Read when you can, listen when you cannot, and let the highlights you make along the way come back to you over time.
